What Is SIFT?

Sift the noise. Find the signal.

Every day, more claims pour in than anyone can check: posts, headlines, screenshots, hot takes. Most of it is noise. SIFT is the machine we run it through — a four-step method for sorting what is true from what is just loud. It is the method behind every Honed Signal report.

The name

What SIFT stands for

SIFT stands for Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, and Trace claims to the original context. Four moves, in roughly that order. None of them takes long. Together they catch most of what makes online information go wrong: the source nobody checked, the quote nobody traced, the story only one outlet is telling.

The method

The four moves

  • 1. Stop

    Don't share it. Don't even finish it. The first move is a pause: do I actually know what this is? A claim that makes you furious, or thrilled, is a claim built to travel — and that is a reason to slow down, not speed up.

    How we do it: nothing goes into a Honed Signal draft the moment it lands. It waits until it has been through the other three moves.

  • 2. Investigate the source

    Before reading what a source says, find out what the source is. Who runs it? What are they selling? What does the rest of the web say about them? A minute of this tells you more than an hour of reading the page itself.

    How we do it: every new source gets checked against wider coverage — including Ground News, which shows how the same story is being reported across the political spectrum.

  • 3. Find better coverage

    If a claim matters, somebody careful has covered it. Don't settle for the version that found you. Go find the best version: the outlet with the documents, the reporter who was actually there, the dataset itself.

    How we do it: no claim makes the cut on one outlet's say-so. We look for the strongest coverage available, and when good coverage disagrees, the report says so.

  • 4. Trace claims to the original context

    Quotes get clipped. Numbers get rounded into different numbers. Photos get borrowed from other events. The last move is to walk the claim back to where it started — the full quote, the original study, the unedited video — and read it there.

    How we do it: a source is a starting point, not a proof. Our reports grade what survives the trace — documented, alleged, or unknown — and say which.

Origin

Where SIFT came from

SIFT was created by Mike Caulfield, a digital literacy researcher at Washington State University. In 2017 he published a free guide, Web Literacy for Student Fact-Checkers, built on a simple observation: professional fact-checkers don't judge a page by reading it longer — they leave it, and check what the rest of the web says about it. He boiled that habit down to four moves, and in 2019 he gave them the name SIFT. Caulfield shares the method freely, and it is now taught in libraries and classrooms all over the country. It is the backbone of every Honed Signal report.

For readers

What this means for readers

When you read a Honed Signal report, this is the work that already happened before the first paragraph. No hype. No noise. Just the signal, with its sources showing.
