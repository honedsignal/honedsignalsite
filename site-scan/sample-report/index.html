What it is: A canonical tag names a page's official address. In October 2026 a sitewide sweep of this site found seven section pages whose canonical tags named other pages — the pages were quietly handing their search credit away.

Why it matters: A page whose canonical points elsewhere generally doesn't rank — the named page does. These were real, finished pages that search engines had been told to ignore.

Example 2 — A copied component that lost its stylesheet (found October 2026 · fixed)

What it is: This site's tool-card component was copied from one hub into six others without the stylesheet that sizes its icons. On iPhone Safari the cards' arrow icons rendered at full container size — a screen-filling arrow where a small one belonged. A later variant of the same class of bug styled card colors only for the first three cards in a grid, leaving every later card's button invisible against the background.

Why it matters: Nothing errored and no link broke — every automated check passed while the pages were visibly broken on a phone. It's the standing reminder behind this scanner: check what visitors actually see, not just what servers answer.

How it was fixed: The component's styles were ported to every page using the markup, icon sizing rules were added for every link context, and the card colors were converted to a repeating cycle so a grid of any size stays styled. The daily monitor now carries a specific check for this class, so it can't come back silently.

Note: this class is specific to this site's own markup, so it's a house check on the daily monitor rather than part of the general report above — included here because it's the best example on record of why the monitor exists.
