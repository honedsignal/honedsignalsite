Before you decide

  • Discount plans usually have no waiting periods or annual caps. Insurance plans often make you wait 6–12 months for major work.
  • The discount must come from participating dentists. Your current dentist may not take the plan — check the provider list first.
  • Insurance plans cover preventive care at 100% — if you only need cleanings, insurance can still lose to a discount plan on price alone.
  • Check what's actually discounted. Discount plans set their own fee schedule per procedure; the % you entered is an estimate.

Procedure prices are typical U.S. mid-range figures (labeled assumptions), not quotes. Insurance math: you pay the deductible first, then your coinsurance share of what's left, with the plan's payments capped at the annual maximum (you pay everything beyond it). Preventive cleanings are assumed covered at 100% if checked. Discount math: you pay the fee, then the discounted price on all procedures. Real plans vary — waiting periods, frequency limits, and in-network requirements all change the real total. This is a planning aid, not insurance or dental advice.
