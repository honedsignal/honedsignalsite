Ways to spend less

  • Compare cost per pill, not bottle price — a $40 bottle can be cheaper than a $20 one.
  • Check auto-ship markups. Subscriptions are convenient, not always cheaper.
  • "Proprietary blends" hide what's actually in the bottle — if you can't verify the dose, you can't verify the value.
  • Buy the dose you take. Mega-bottles only save money if you finish them before they expire.

This tool tracks cost only — it makes no claims about whether any supplement works, is safe, or is worth taking. Cost per pill is bottle price divided by pill count; cost per day is cost per pill times your daily dose. Prices vary by retailer and over time, so treat these as your entered snapshot. Supplements can interact with medications and with each other — talk to a doctor or pharmacist about interactions and whether you need any of these at all.
