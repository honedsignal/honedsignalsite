What “% of poverty level” means

  • 100% is the poverty line itself — for a single person in most states, $15,960 in 2026.
  • 200% is double the poverty line. Many nonprofit hospitals write off bills entirely below this line.
  • 400% is four times the line. Many hospitals offer sliding-scale discounts up to here.
  • These are typical thresholds, not rules. Every hospital sets its own policy — some are more generous.

Important: this is eligibility information, not a benefits determination. Only the hospital’s financial assistance office can decide what you actually qualify for — call them and ask.

This tool divides your annual household income by the 2026 HHS federal poverty guideline for your household size (Alaska and Hawaii use their own higher figures) and reports the percentage. The 200% and 400% cutoffs are widely cited typical hospital charity-care thresholds — many nonprofit hospitals offer free care under about 200% of the poverty level and discounted care up to about 400%. They are typical, not universal: each hospital publishes its own financial assistance policy, and some help people above 400%. This page explains the math; it does not determine your eligibility. Confirm with the hospital’s financial assistance office.
