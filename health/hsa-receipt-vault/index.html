How the no-deadline rule works

  • Pay out of pocket now and keep the receipt — your HSA balance keeps growing tax-free.
  • Reimburse yourself whenever: next year, in ten years, in retirement. Under current IRS rules there is no time limit.
  • Keep the actual receipts somewhere safe. This vault is an index — it can't replace the receipt if the IRS asks.
  • Mark entries reimbursed as you pay yourself back so your available total stays honest.

Expense log

Newest first. Check “Reimbursed” when you've paid yourself back.

  • No time limit — for now. Under current IRS rules there is no deadline to reimburse yourself for qualified HSA expenses. Tax rules can change, so verify before relying on this years from now.
  • Only qualified expenses count. The expense must be a qualified medical expense incurred after your HSA was established. Expenses from before you opened the HSA don't qualify.
  • This log is an index, not a receipt. Keep the actual receipts — paper or photos — somewhere safe. If the IRS asks for proof, this log alone won't cut it.
  • Don't confuse this with an FSA. Flexible Spending Accounts generally have use-it-or-lose-it deadlines. HSAs don't work that way — but only if you keep the records.
  • This is not tax advice. It's a record-keeping tool. For your situation, talk to a tax professional.
