Ways to pay less

  • 90-day supplies often cost less per pill than 30-day fills.
  • Ask your doctor about therapeutic alternatives — a different drug in the same class can cost far less.
  • Brand-name drugs: check the manufacturer's patient assistance program; many are income-based and free.
  • Cash can beat insurance. If your copay is higher than a coupon price, you can usually pay cash instead.

This tool ranks the prices you enter — it does not look up live prices. Drug prices vary by pharmacy, location, and day, so re-check before you fill. Discount coupons (like GoodRx) generally cannot be combined with insurance; compare both and pay whichever is lower. Never switch, split, or stop a prescription to save money without talking to your prescriber or pharmacist. If cost forces a choice, ask your doctor about therapeutic alternatives rather than skipping doses.
