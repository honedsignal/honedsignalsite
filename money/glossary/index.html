Financial Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used across Honed Money's calculators. Jump to a letter, or just scroll — no jargon, no footnotes needed.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

A

Amortization
The process of paying off a loan in fixed monthly payments over time, with each payment split between interest and the loan balance.
Used in: Auto Loan Calculator, Mortgage Payment Calculator
Annuity
A series of fixed payments made over time. The classic example is a pension: take it as a lump sum, or as monthly payments for life.
Used in: Pension Lump Sum vs. Annuity Calculator, Retirement Income Gap Calculator
APR
Annual percentage rate: the yearly cost of borrowing expressed as a percent, including interest and certain fees, so you can compare loans apples to apples.
Used in: Auto Loan Calculator, Credit Card Payoff Analyzer
APY
Annual percentage yield: the yearly return on savings after accounting for compounding. Higher APY means your money grows faster.
Used in: Compound Interest Calculator, Emergency Fund Calculator

B

Basis point
One one-hundredth of a percent (0.01%). Finance folks use it to talk about small changes — "rates rose 25 basis points" means rates went up a quarter of a percent.
Used in: Investment Return Calculator, Mortgage Scenario Comparison

C

Capital gain
The profit you make when you sell an asset like stock for more than you paid. Gains on assets held over a year are taxed at lower long-term rates.
Used in: Capital Gains Tax Calculator, Investment Return Calculator
COBRA
A federal law that lets you keep your employer's health plan for a limited time after leaving a job — but you pay the full premium yourself.
Used in: COBRA vs. Marketplace Calculator
Coinsurance
The percentage of a medical bill you pay after meeting your deductible. With 20% coinsurance, you pay one-fifth and your plan pays the rest.
Used in: Out-of-Pocket Health Cost Estimator, HDHP vs. PPO Calculator
Compound interest
Interest earned on both your original amount and the interest you've already earned. It's the snowball effect that makes money grow faster the longer it's invested.
Used in: Compound Interest Calculator, Dividend Reinvestment Calculator
Copay
A fixed amount you pay for a covered health service — say, $30 per doctor visit — usually without needing to meet your deductible first.
Used in: Out-of-Pocket Health Cost Estimator, HDHP vs. PPO Calculator

D

Deductible (insurance)
The amount you pay out of pocket each year before your insurance starts paying for covered services.
Used in: Auto Insurance Deductible Calculator, Homeowners Insurance Estimator
Depreciation
The drop in an asset's value over time from wear or age — a car losing value each year, for example. Businesses can also deduct depreciation on their taxes.
Used in: Car Ownership Cost Calculator
Discount points
Optional upfront fees paid to a lender to lower your mortgage rate. One point usually costs 1% of the loan and cuts the rate by about a quarter of a percent.
Used in: Mortgage Discount Points Calculator, Refinance Break-Even Calculator
Diversification
Spreading investments across different assets so one bad pick can't sink your whole portfolio. Don't put all your eggs in one basket.
Used in: Portfolio Allocation Calculator

E

Emergency fund
Cash set aside for unexpected expenses like job loss or car repairs. Most advisers suggest 3–6 months of essential spending.
Used in: Emergency Fund Calculator
Equity
What you truly own. Your home equity is your home's value minus the mortgage; stock equity is ownership in a company.
Used in: HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan Calculator, Net Worth Calculator
Escrow
An account where a third party holds money during a deal — like the portion of your mortgage payment set aside for property taxes and insurance.
Used in: Mortgage Payment Calculator, Closing Cost Calculator

F

FSA
Flexible spending account: a pre-tax account through work for medical expenses. Most FSAs are "use it or lose it" — unspent money disappears at year end.
Used in: FSA vs. HSA Calculator

H

HDHP
High-deductible health plan: lower monthly premiums in exchange for a high deductible. Often paired with an HSA for tax-free medical savings.
Used in: HDHP vs. PPO Calculator, HSA Tax Savings Calculator
HELOC
Home equity line of credit: a credit line secured by your home, with a variable rate, that you draw on and repay like a credit card.
Used in: HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan Calculator
HSA
Health savings account: a tax-advantaged account for medical costs tied to an HDHP. Contributions are tax-deductible, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for medical expenses are tax-free.
Used in: HSA Tax Savings Calculator, FSA vs. HSA Calculator

I

IDR (income-driven repayment)
A federal student loan plan that caps monthly payments at a share of your discretionary income and forgives the remaining balance after 20–25 years.
Used in: Student Loan Refi vs. IDR Calculator, Student Loan Payoff Calculator
Inflation
The general rise in prices over time, which shrinks what each dollar buys. A dollar today won't buy as much in ten years.
Used in: Inflation Calculator, Retirement Savings Projector
IRA (traditional and Roth)
Individual retirement account. A traditional IRA gives you a tax break now and taxes withdrawals in retirement; a Roth IRA taxes contributions now and lets qualified withdrawals come out tax-free.
Used in: Roth vs. Traditional IRA Calculator, HSA vs. IRA Comparison Calculator

L

Lump sum
A single one-time payment instead of payments spread over time — like taking a pension as one check versus monthly payments for life.
Used in: Pension Lump Sum vs. Annuity Calculator

M

Marginal tax rate
The tax rate on your next dollar of income. A raise doesn't get "all taxed higher" — only the dollars that land in the higher bracket are taxed at that rate.
Used in: Effective vs. Marginal Tax Rate Calculator, Tax Bracket Calculator
Medigap
Supplemental insurance that covers costs Original Medicare doesn't, like copays and deductibles. It only works alongside Original Medicare.
Used in: Medicare Retiree Health Cost Estimator

N

Net worth
What you own minus what you owe. Savings and home value count as assets; loans and credit card balances count as liabilities.
Used in: Net Worth Calculator

O

Out-of-pocket maximum
The most you'll pay in a year for covered health care. Once you hit it, your plan covers 100% of covered services for the rest of the year.
Used in: Out-of-Pocket Health Cost Estimator, HDHP vs. PPO Calculator

P

PPO
Preferred provider organization: a health plan that pays more when you use in-network doctors but still covers out-of-network care at a higher cost to you.
Used in: HDHP vs. PPO Calculator
Premium (insurance)
The amount you pay regularly to keep an insurance policy active, usually monthly — whether or not you ever file a claim.
Used in: Term vs. Whole Life Insurance Calculator, Umbrella Insurance Calculator
Principal
The original amount borrowed or invested, before interest. On a mortgage, early payments go mostly to interest, with the principal paid down over time.
Used in: Mortgage Payment Calculator, Auto Loan Calculator

R

Refinance
Replacing an old loan with a new one, usually to get a lower rate or shorter term. Closing costs mean there's a break-even point before you actually save money.
Used in: Refinance Break-Even Calculator, Mortgage Payment Calculator
Required minimum distribution (RMD)
The yearly withdrawal the IRS requires from traditional retirement accounts starting at age 73 or 75 (depending on your birth year), taxed as ordinary income.
Used in: RMD Calculator, Retirement Savings Projector
Roth conversion
Moving money from a traditional IRA or 401(k) into a Roth IRA. You pay income tax on the amount now, but future qualified withdrawals come out tax-free.
Used in: Roth Conversion Calculator, Tax Bracket Calculator

S

Standard deduction
A flat dollar amount you can subtract from taxable income instead of itemizing deductions. Most filers take it because it's larger and simpler.
Used in: Standard vs. Itemized Deduction Calculator, Tax Bracket Calculator

T

Term life insurance
Life insurance that covers a set number of years. It's the cheapest way to get a large death benefit, but there's no cash value if you outlive the term.
Used in: Term vs. Whole Life Insurance Calculator, Life Insurance Needs Calculator

U

Umbrella insurance
Extra liability coverage that kicks in when your auto or home policy limits run out, protecting your assets in big lawsuits.
Used in: Umbrella Insurance Calculator

V

Vesting
Earning full ownership of employer contributions, like a 401(k) match, over time. Leave before you're vested and you forfeit the unvested portion.
Used in: 401(k) Match Calculator

W

Withholding
Income tax your employer takes out of each paycheck and sends to the IRS. Too little withheld means a tax bill in April; too much means a refund of your own money.
Used in: W-4 Withholding Calculator, Take-Home Pay Calculator
