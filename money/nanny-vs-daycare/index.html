The nanny tax

Don’t skip the nanny tax

If you pay a household employee $2,800 or more in a year (2026 threshold), you owe the employer share of Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) plus federal and state unemployment insurance. That is roughly 10% on top of wages. Paying cash “off the books” is illegal and can cost you back taxes and penalties if caught. The tax cost above is already included in the nanny total.

Planning note: Daycare typically charges per child, so its cost grows with family size; a nanny’s wages are largely flat whether you have one child or three. Employer tax figures here are an estimate — actual rates vary by state. Cost is only one factor: schedules, sick-child policies, and socialization matter too.
