Explain these results

  • HDHP: A high-deductible health plan usually trades lower premiums for more cost before coverage kicks in. If it meets federal rules, it may let you contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA), where eligible contributions and withdrawals receive tax advantages.
  • PPO: A Preferred Provider Organization usually offers a broad network and some out-of-network coverage, often at a higher price. “PPO” describes the network, so a plan can technically be both a PPO and a high-deductible plan.
  • This estimate assumes you first pay the deductible, then the listed coinsurance, until the out-of-pocket maximum. Real plans may use copays or separate rules for prescriptions, office visits, and out-of-network care.
  • The lower estimate is not automatically the better plan. Also compare the provider network, drug coverage, employer HSA contributions, and how comfortable you are with a large bill early in the year.
