Explain these results

Term life covers you for a set number of years. It is usually the lower-cost way to buy a larger death benefit during working years, while raising children, or while paying a mortgage. It normally has no cash value, and coverage ends unless you renew or replace it.

Whole life is designed to last for life if required premiums are paid. Part of the premium supports a cash-value account that can grow over time. It usually costs more, and using or borrowing against cash value can reduce the death benefit.

This comparison adds up premiums over the term length you entered. It does not estimate cash-value growth, fees, dividends, taxes, premium changes, or the value of coverage beyond that period. Ask for policy illustrations and compare the guarantees—not just projections—before deciding.
