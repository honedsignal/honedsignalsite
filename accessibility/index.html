Accessibility

No hype. No noise.

How this site is built to be usable by everyone we can reach, what still falls short, and how to tell us. Last updated November 8, 2026.

Commitment

Built to be usable, checked in the open

Honed Signal is a hand-built, static website — no app to install and no account wall — and we aim for the WCAG 2.1 AA guidelines as the target for new and updated pages. Accessibility fits this site’s whole premise: research nobody can read isn’t finished.

In place now

What the pages already do

  • Structure: real headings in order, a skip-to-content link, and pages that read in a sensible order without styling.
  • Images: informative images carry text alternatives; the characters and artwork are described where they carry meaning.
  • Contrast and focus: the dark theme pairs light text on deep navy, links and buttons are reachable by keyboard, and focus is visible.
  • No surprises: nothing autoplays, flashes, or moves on a timer.
  • Plain language: short sentences and explained terms are a house rule, not an afterthought.
  • JavaScript off: core content stays readable. One exception by design: email links are assembled by a small script so scrapers can’t harvest the address — but the address itself is printed as text, contact@honedsignal.com, so it can be copied either way.

Known limits

Where we still fall short

  • Some older tool pages were built before these standards were written down and may not meet every point above yet; they are brought up to standard as they are revised.
  • Printable PDF products are designed for print first and may not work well with screen readers.
  • Some report evidence — records, filings, screenshots — cannot always carry a complete text equivalent.
  • Embedded third-party players (such as YouTube) are governed by that platform’s accessibility features.

Tell us

Hit a barrier? We want the report

Email contact@honedsignal.com with the page and the problem — the device and assistive technology you’re using help us reproduce it. We fix what we can, substantive fixes are recorded in the corrections log, and if a page or document won’t work with your technology, contact us and we will find another way to get you the same content.
